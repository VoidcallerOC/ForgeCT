## 2. Blind identity test (logo, name, colours, client names removed)

What survives the test: the parking-lot test (Q1–Q4), the phone rail of real storefronts, the work ledger as a table, the rate sheet as one spec table, "one person, start to finish", the Nino's case study with dated honesty. Those are specific to this studio.

What does not survive: a dark, monochrome, tight-tracked display-type site with an **ambient starfield canvas**, a **three-number stat strip** ("6 / 3 / 24h", where "Build tiers 3" is filler), an **"Available" status dot**, and an audit that is **named** "the digital inspection ticket" but **rendered** as a plain form under plain text. That combination reads as a contemporary premium-dev-studio template.

## 6. Blind test, after

1. Built for real local businesses? Yes: towns, storefront phones, parking-lot questions, a paper inspection ticket.
2. Authentic evidence? Yes: six live sites, screenshots of the real pages, deliverables stated without invented results, dated status where parts are not live.
3. Distinct journey? See the work → read the ticket → send a URL → decide. One builder throughout.
4. Audit identity of its own? The ticket is now an object you would recognise with the logo hidden.
5. Price and next step in seconds? Three prices in the chooser and one spec table; "Book a session about Basic" per column; audit first everywhere.
6. Could it be mistaken for a generic AI agency template? Less so. The remaining generic signal is the dark-mode, large-display-type baseline itself, which is the established forge-ct.com brand and was kept on purpose. The homepage's triple listing of the same six clients (A-09) is the next thing to decide.
